Behind the telescope

I'm Islam Urazov.

I'm from Nukus, (Karakalpakstan) Uzbekistan, and live in the Netherlands. This is where I share my astrophotography and my curiosity about the universe.

QARA TUYNIK brings those interests together: photographs of the night sky, films made from my images, and an exploration of black holes.

From Nukus to the stars

As a child growing up in Nukus, Karakalpakstan, I dreamed of becoming a cosmonaut. I collected astronomy books and tried to understand the distant worlds above me.

I remember watching rocket trails stretch across the sky—flights from Baikonur leaving beautiful traces near the horizon. Seeing them made space feel closer, like somewhere people could actually go.

Some of my clearest memories are of returning home late at night after visiting relatives. Above us, the stars shone brightly, and the Milky Way stretched across the sky. I would look up and wonder how far it went, and what might be waiting out there.

Today, living in the Netherlands, I explore that same sky through a telescope and a camera. Learning to photograph nebulae and galaxies has given my childhood curiosity a new direction.

QARA TUYNIK is where I share that journey—from the skies I remember over Karakalpakstan to the light I collect from my garden today.

A window into the unknown

Darkness at the centre.
Light all around.

A black hole gives off no light from within its event horizon. What we can see is its influence: matter heating as it spirals inward, and light taking curved paths through the space around it.

01 — THE BOUNDARY

Event horizon

The boundary beyond which light cannot escape. The apparent dark shadow is larger than the horizon itself.

02 — THE GLOW

Accretion disk

Gas orbiting a black hole can become intensely hot. The luminous disk reveals matter in motion around an otherwise invisible object.

03 — THE DISTORTION

Gravitational lensing

Gravity bends the paths of light. Parts of the far side of a disk can appear above and below the black hole.
